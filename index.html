Fit Me Baby One More Time!

About

A recurring problem across scientific and technical domains is turning a sample of observations into an underlying probability distribution. Irrespective of whether you are modelling the energy spectrum of a particle decay, the arrival times of photons on a detector, the returns of a financial instrument, or the scattering lobes used in path guiding and BxDF material representations, you ultimately always arrive at the same question:
How does the distribution that generated these samples look like?

The answer is rarely obvious. Real data are noisy, skewed, multimodal, and/or censored, and the model you choose shapes every conclusion drawn from it. A generic default such as a plain Gaussian is easy to justify, but often a very simplified, if not poor description of the data at hand. Hence we are so interested in developing a model that can accomplish at least one property (of course, ideally more than just one) better than what the current state of the art offers, whether that is stability, expressiveness, speed, or something entirely different.

FMBOMT (Fit Me Baby One More Time) is a small utility for the exploration of such properties. It lets you visually compose a target distribution, draw samples from it, and fit a range of models to those samples, comparing how well each recovers the ground truth you planted. Additionally, you may implement your own distribution to use for fitting the data. Additional features include live-updating model parameters, switching between one and two-dimensional representations, as well as defining the space you want to operate in. Once finished, export publication-ready figures under CC0. Please let me know via the link down below if you encounter any issues throughout the use, and most importantly, have fun!
